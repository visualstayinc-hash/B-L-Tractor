# Concept — Heating & Air Conditioning Amarillo

**Thesis:** Control the Climate. The site makes an invisible HVAC system visible — air moves, heat moves, cold moves, and the interface visibly responds to user input, the same way a serviced system responds in a real home.

**Visual system:** temperature gradients, curved airflow lines (SVG, draw-in on load), a simplified duct/vent schematic, condensation/heat-shimmer as literal states of a live control — not decoration. Palette: cool electric blue, controlled heat orange, neutral charcoal/paper. No photography (none was supplied) — nothing fabricated to imply real technicians, trucks, or job photos.

**Structure:**
1. Hero — static, identity + CTAs visible immediately on load; curved cool/heat airflow lines draw in, a simplified HVAC unit locks into place off to the side (not overlapping the text).
2. Temperature Control (signature interaction) — a real range input (60–85°) that changes the stage's background color, airflow line color/speed, and (at the cold end) condensation particles in real time. Genuine state change, not a decorative slider.
3. System — Heat/Air/Cool mode selector driving a duct/vent schematic's flow color and a moving airflow dot, paired with plain-language "air in → conditioned → comfort out" steps.
4. Problem/Solution — No Heat / Too Hot / Airflow Problem / System Failed chips mapping to a service category, explicitly framed as a general guide, not a diagnosis.
5. Reviews — 4.4★/7 reviews shown honestly, including the negative review ("hung up on") disclosed up front rather than hidden. Positive reviews browsable by category (Response/Repair/Quality/Professionalism), each the client's exact supplied text.
6. Contact — Call/Directions, address, phone, honest "call to confirm hours" (the client's Google snippet was a same-day dynamic line, not a verified weekly schedule).

**Facts discipline:** business name and phone number used exactly as the client supplied them, even though research surfaced a plausible different formal business name ("Premier Heating & Air") and a different phone number in wider use elsewhere — that identity question wasn't mine to resolve unilaterally, so it was flagged to the client rather than acted on. "Art" and "Jorge" appear only inside the client's own quoted reviews. No address invention (none needed — client gave one). No service claims beyond what the reviews support (heating, furnace repair, preventive maintenance, HVAC installation, air conditioning service). No fabricated license number, founding year, or expanded service list, since those were contingent on an identity match the client hasn't confirmed.
